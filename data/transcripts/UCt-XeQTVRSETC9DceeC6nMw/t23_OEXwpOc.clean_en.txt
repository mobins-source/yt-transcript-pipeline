In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious , the Most Merciful. Praise be to Allah , and peace and blessings be upon the Messenger of Allah. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and messenger. O Allah, send prayers, peace, and blessings upon our Prophet Muhammad, and upon his family and all his companions. To proceed: The author says, "He used to," meaning the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace. He would lean on his palms, spread them out, bring his fingers together, and direct them toward the Qiblah. Meaning, in prostration, when he reached the ground after first placing his noble knees down, he would lean on his palms, spread them out—not clench them—and keep his fingers together. He said, "And he would direct them toward the Qiblah." Meaning, in prostration, the fingers should be this. Not this, not this, and not this. this, and toward the Qiblah. Because Allah, the Almighty, commanded us to face the Qiblah. He said, "Turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque." So, facing the Qiblah with everything possible is within the scope and domain of the command, as is well known in... the science of Usul (principles of.

Shoulders. This is the shoulder. Level with his shoulders, and sometimes level with his ears. Meaning, in prostration, he would place ... his hands this in prostration, or this in prostration. He would direct his fingers toward the Qiblah and firmly place his nose and forehead on...the ground. It is necessary to firmly place both the forehead and the nose. Scholars say that whoever prostrates without firmly placing their nose on the ground, their prostration is flawed or invalid. The prostration might be invalidated by this. You find many people prostrating here, prostrating here. On their head, right here. Until they get that mark there. This is wrong; this is evidence of an incorrect prostration. Rather, the Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, used to prostrate here, right here. The nose and forehead here, because it is not the head that prostrates, but the face that prostrates. "My face has prostrated to the One who created it and fashioned its hearing and vision by His power and strength." And the Almighty says, "And they fall upon their faces weeping."(Lit: they fall upon their chins in prostration). Meaning, were it not for the nose, the chin would touch the ground. Falling upon their chins is an exaggeration of humility before Allah; they fall upon their chins in prostration. This is how Allah describes the people of knowledge.
